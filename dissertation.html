Analysing British Aristocratic Intermarriages

An end-to-end pipeline that turns 39,517 historical biographies into a queryable data warehouse and a model of who intermarried within the British aristocracy.

The problem

Unstructured history, no analysis

The Oxford Dictionary of National Biography holds rich life records, but only as XML text. No one had warehoused or analysed its intermarriage data for patterns. Researchers in the School of Law and Social Sciences needed a structured, queryable source.

The data

39,517 XML biographies

Each record holds names, dates, places, education, occupations and spouses in free text. The data had many missing values and only 1,033 intermarried people against 19,875 who were not.

Approach

CRISP-DM, end to end

  1. Extract. Parsed XML with ElementTree and regex into structured fields.
  2. Clean. Imputed or removed missing values, removed duplicates and detected outliers with IQR.
  3. Engineer. Grouped free-text occupations using TF-IDF and K-Means, and reduced dimensions with TruncatedSVD.
  4. Warehouse. Designed a star schema and loaded it into SQL Server with SQLAlchemy, pyodbc and stored procedures.
  5. Model. Trained a Decision Tree tuned with GridSearchCV and a Random Forest rebalanced with SMOTE.
  6. Visualise. Built an interactive network of marriage links with PyVis.
Results

What the models showed

Decision paths showed gender, occupation cluster, birth place and death year as the most influential features.

Next steps

What I would improve

Richer kinship features, alternative resampling methods and database stress testing would strengthen the model and the warehouse. A public portal for researchers was out of scope and remains a natural next phase.
