The problem

Five business questions

The brigade wanted to know how false alarms waste resources, where secondary fires cluster, how location delays the first engine, whether incident type can be predicted at call time, and where a slow second engine raises cost.

The data

5,537 incidents, 39 attributes

Each incident records call time, property type, postcode, ward, attending stations, response times, pump hours and notional cost. I removed redundant columns, imputed missing values and removed outliers with IQR and Z-scores.

Approach

One method per question

Results

What the data showed

  1. False alarms are the biggest cost. They made up 2,890 incidents and about £1.09M of £2.72M total notional cost.
  2. They peak in the daytime. Clustering split the day in two, with far more false alarms after 08:00.
  3. Secondary fires have hotspots. KT1, KT2 and KT3 were linked to afternoon secondary fires, and KT2 and KT6 to mornings.
  4. Incident type is predictable. Logistic regression predicted the incident group with 72% accuracy from call time, property type and location.
Next steps

What I would improve

Adding traffic, road network and station distance data would explain response delays better. The ward-level link between second-engine delay and cost was not clear and needs more data.
