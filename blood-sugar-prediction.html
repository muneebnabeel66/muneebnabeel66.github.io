The problem

Warn patients before risk

People with Type 1 diabetes need to know if their glucose will go too low or too high soon. The aim was to forecast the next two hours and classify each forecast as hypoglycemic, normal or hyperglycemic, without using any future glucose data.

The data

4,320 five-minute readings

Simulated adult, adolescent and child patients from the FDA-approved UVA-Padova simulator, with continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), carbohydrate intake and insulin, from 25 to 30 October.

Approach

A rolling forecast loop

  1. Prepare. Converted timestamps, scaled features with Min-Max scaling and scaled the glucose thresholds.
  2. Forecast with ARIMA. Tuned the order and chose ARIMA(5, 1, 0).
  3. Forecast with Prophet. Added carbohydrate intake as an extra regressor.
  4. Retrain continuously. Retrained on all past data at each step and predicted two hours ahead.
  5. Classify and validate. Classified each forecast into a risk state and refined Prophet with rolling cross-validation.
Results

Prophet clearly wins

Prophet handled the irregular glucose pattern and meal effects far better than ARIMA.

Next steps

What I would improve

Using insulin as a further regressor, testing on real patient data and trying gradient-boosted or deep learning models would be the next steps.
